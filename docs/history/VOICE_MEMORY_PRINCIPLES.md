# ArchiveMe Product Principles

Permanent rules for a long-term personal archive. When in doubt, choose silence, continuity, and trust.

## Core principles

1. **Silence over filler** — Absence is preferable to weak copy. One grounded line beats three explanatory ones.
2. **Continuity over novelty** — Revisit what already mattered. Do not chase fresh emotional angles for engagement.
3. **Emotional legitimacy over cleverness** — Copy must be earned from behavior and entries, not invented for effect.
4. **Archive permanence over engagement** — The product exists to be kept for years, not to maximize session time.
5. **Trust over optimization** — Never trade emotional safety for metrics, conversion, or retention hacks.
6. **Emotional accuracy over frequency** — Fewer true observations beat frequent generic ones.
7. **No productivity framing** — Not a journal habit app, not self-improvement, not a growth tool.
8. **No emotional manipulation** — No fear, urgency, scarcity, or guilt to drive action.
9. **No growth hacking psychology** — No streaks, levels, badges, FOMO, or “don't miss” language.

## Good vs bad copy

| Good | Bad |
|------|-----|
| “You returned here months later.” | “Don't miss this breakthrough moment.” |
| “Your archive can stay with you.” | “Unlock your full potential.” |
| “Encrypted backup keeps this recoverable.” | “Upgrade now before you lose everything.” |
| “This belonged to a heavier stretch.” | “You're entering a growth phase.” |
| “Some people choose to protect their archive long-term.” | “Join thousands of users.” |
| (silence — no note shown) | “Daily habit streak: 7 days!” |

---

## Forbidden framing

Never use:

- Productivity: habit, streak, goal, optimize, productivity, daily practice, level up
- Therapy/coaching: healing journey, inner work, breakthrough, transform, best self
- Manipulation: don't miss, hurry, limited time, last chance, fear of losing, never lose
- Growth SaaS: scale, viral, waitlist, exclusive access, game changer, users like you
- Achievement: badge, milestone unlocked, top memory, most important reflection
- Intelligence upsell: premium intelligence, AI insights, unlock features, smarter memories

---

## Emotional restraint rules

- Max one primary emotional note per surface per session.
- Suppress when emotional moment just occurred or revisit payoff is active.
- Suppress weak, generic, or template-feeling callbacks.
- Prefer temporal grounding (“around that time”, “before things settled”) over psychology.
- No diagnostic or interpretive labels (Phase 1, trauma, healing era).
- Callbacks must survive usefulness and orientation filters before display.

---

## Monetization boundaries

Payment may only be framed as:

- Archive continuity
- Encrypted backup
- Preservation and portability
- Sustainable careful development

Never:

- Feature unlocks
- More intelligence or insights
- Productivity or optimization
- Urgency or scarcity
- Automatic checkout without founder approval in pilot phase

---

## Sharing boundaries

Sharing must be:

- Quiet, optional, user-initiated
- Grounded in a specific line the user chose
- Free of performative or viral framing

Never:

- Share prompts during emotional peaks
- Social proof that implies popularity
- Creator/growth monetization language

---

## Revisit philosophy

Revisits should feel like:

- Returning to something already owned
- Noticing continuity, not being coached
- Optional depth, not required engagement

Avoid:

- Revisit streaks or rewards
- “You came back!” celebration
- Forcing revisit → reflection → follow-up chains
- Fatigue from adjacent emotional reopenings

---

## Archive permanence philosophy

The archive should feel:

- Portable (export anytime)
- Durable (survives years, migration, device changes)
- Personally irreplaceable through attachment, not lock-in
- Emotionally safe to keep private

Never:

- Hostage tactics (“your memories are trapped here”)
- Deletion fear marketing
- Rankings of “most important” memories
- Gamified depth scores

---

## When adding features, ask

1. Does this increase trust or noise?
2. Could this be removed without hurting attachment?
3. Will this still feel calm after three years?
4. Is this explaining too much?

If systems overlap heavily: **the archive may be becoming overdesigned.**

If emotional density rises: **the product may be explaining too much.**
